# Offsite Risk and Contingency Plan

This fictional example shows how I think through common offsite risks before they become day-of problems.

The goal is not to predict every possible issue. It is to identify the risks most likely to disrupt the event, decide what can be handled independently, and know when leadership needs to be involved.

## Travel Disruptions

### Risk

Flight delays, cancellations, missed connections, or late arrivals.

### Preparation

- Track attendee arrival and departure details
- Keep hotel and transportation information in one place
- Identify alternate flights when travel is especially tight
- Confirm late check-in procedures with the hotel
- Keep transportation vendors updated on major schedule changes

### Response

Handle routine rebooking and transportation changes directly.

Escalate only if the disruption affects a major meeting, creates a significant cost issue, or requires a change to the executive schedule.

## Vendor Cancellation or Failure

### Risk

A transportation provider, restaurant, activity vendor, or other supplier cancels or fails to deliver.

### Preparation

- Keep backup options for high-impact vendors
- Maintain direct vendor contact information
- Review cancellation policies in advance
- Confirm critical vendors again one week before and 24 to 48 hours before the event

### Response

Move to the backup option when possible.

Escalate if the replacement significantly changes cost, experience, or schedule.

## Weather

### Risk

Weather affects travel, transportation, or outdoor activities.

### Preparation

- Monitor forecasts leading into the event
- Identify indoor alternatives for weather-sensitive activities
- Build reasonable transition time into the schedule
- Confirm vendor weather policies

### Response

Make the switch to the backup plan when the impact is operational.

Escalate only when leadership needs to choose between materially different options.

## Catering or Dietary Issue

### Risk

A dietary restriction is missed, headcount changes, or catering delivery is delayed.

### Preparation

- Collect dietary needs early
- Reconfirm final headcount
- Keep dietary requirements documented with the caterer
- Identify nearby backup food options

### Response

Work directly with the caterer or source an alternative.

Keep the attendee experience moving without involving leadership unless the issue affects the broader event.

## AV or Technology Failure

### Risk

Presentation equipment, internet access, conferencing tools, or meeting-room technology fails.

### Preparation

- Confirm AV requirements in advance
- Test equipment before important sessions
- Keep presentation materials available in more than one format
- Have charging equipment and adapters available
- Know who the venue AV contact is

### Response

Troubleshoot with the venue or move to the backup setup.

Escalate only if the issue materially affects the meeting or requires a schedule change.

## Agenda Overrun

### Risk

Meetings run long and begin affecting meals, transportation, activities, or later sessions.

### Preparation

- Build transition time into the schedule
- Know which agenda items are flexible
- Keep vendors informed when timing is especially important
- Identify what can be shortened, moved, or removed if needed

### Response

Adjust lower-priority logistics and communicate changes.

Protect the highest-value leadership sessions where possible.

## Executive Schedule Change

### Risk

An executive needs to join another call, leave early, arrive late, or change priorities during the offsite.

### Preparation

- Keep the executive schedule separate from the broader attendee schedule
- Know which sessions require executive participation
- Build flexibility into less critical portions of the agenda
- Keep key stakeholders informed without creating unnecessary disruption

### Response

Rework the schedule around the highest-priority executive commitments.

Handle downstream communication and logistics directly.

## Contract or Payment Issue

### Risk

A vendor invoice, deposit, payment deadline, or contract term becomes an issue close to the event.

### Preparation

- Track payment deadlines
- Review contracts and cancellation windows
- Keep approval requirements clear
- Confirm who can authorize changes

### Response

Resolve routine administrative issues directly.

Escalate when there is a meaningful financial, legal, or contractual decision.

## Attendee Emergency

### Risk

An attendee becomes ill, has a personal emergency, or needs unexpected support.

### Preparation

- Maintain emergency contact information when appropriate
- Know the nearest urgent care or emergency facility
- Keep hotel and transportation contacts available
- Protect attendee privacy

### Response

Handle immediate logistics with discretion and care.

Escalate only to the people who genuinely need to know.

## Decision Framework

When something goes wrong, I ask:

1. Can I resolve this within the existing plan and budget?
2. Does this affect the executive schedule?
3. Does it materially affect attendee experience?
4. Does it create a significant financial, legal, or reputational issue?
5. Does leadership actually need to make a decision?

If the answer to the last question is no, I handle it.

## Why This Matters

A strong offsite plan should not require leadership to manage logistics when something changes.

The better the preparation, the more problems can be solved quietly without disrupting the purpose of the event.
