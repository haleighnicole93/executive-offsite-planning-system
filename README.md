# Executive Offsite Planning System

A portfolio example of how I structure and manage a multi-day executive or team offsite from initial planning through final execution.

## The Problem

Company offsites involve a large number of moving parts that can easily become scattered across email threads, spreadsheets, vendor conversations, calendars, and individual task lists.

The challenge is not just booking travel or choosing a venue. It is keeping every dependency visible, making sure decisions happen on time, and preventing small logistical details from becoming last-minute problems.

## What This System Covers

This planning system is designed to organize the full lifecycle of an offsite, including:

- Venue and hotel coordination
- Travel planning
- Vendor management
- Budget tracking
- Attendee logistics
- Meeting and activity scheduling
- Meal planning
- Contracts and payment deadlines
- Internal communications
- Executive preferences
- Contingency planning
- Final confirmations
- Day-of execution

## How I Approach the Project

I break the event into phases so that decisions and dependencies are handled in the right order.

### Phase 1: Scope and Requirements

Define:

- Purpose of the offsite
- Dates
- Attendee count
- Location options
- Budget
- Executive priorities
- Meeting requirements
- Travel expectations
- Team activities
- Accessibility or dietary needs

### Phase 2: Vendor and Location Planning

Research and coordinate:

- Hotels
- Meeting venues
- Transportation
- Catering
- Restaurants
- Activities
- Audio and visual needs
- Local vendors

Track proposals, contracts, deadlines, deposits, and final payment dates in one place.

### Phase 3: Attendee Logistics

Organize:

- Travel information
- Arrival and departure times
- Room assignments
- Ground transportation
- Dietary restrictions
- Emergency contact information
- Special accommodations

### Phase 4: Schedule and Dependencies

Build the master schedule and identify which items depend on another decision being completed first.

Examples:

- Final venue selection before hotel room block
- Attendee count before catering guarantees
- Travel details before airport transportation
- Agenda completion before room setup
- Contract approval before deposits are paid

## How I Measure Success

A successful offsite means:

- Executives are not pulled into unnecessary logistics
- Attendees know exactly where they need to be
- Vendors are confirmed and paid on time
- Important deadlines are visible before they become urgent
- The event stays within the approved budget
- Last-minute changes can be handled without disrupting the larger plan
- Leadership can focus on the purpose of the offsite instead of the mechanics behind it
