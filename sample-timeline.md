# Sample Offsite Timeline

This fictional example shows how I sequence major offsite decisions, dependencies, and deadlines so the project stays ahead of problems instead of reacting to them.

## 8 to 10 Weeks Before

### Confirm the Foundation

- Confirm event goals and success criteria
- Confirm dates
- Confirm attendee count
- Establish budget
- Narrow location options
- Identify executive priorities
- Determine meeting and activity requirements
- Begin hotel and venue research

### Key Dependency

The location and budget need to be approved before contracts, deposits, and major travel planning can move forward.

## 6 to 8 Weeks Before

### Lock Major Vendors

- Sign hotel agreement
- Sign meeting venue agreement
- Confirm initial room block
- Review cancellation terms
- Track deposits and payment deadlines
- Begin collecting attendee travel details
- Begin collecting dietary and accessibility needs

### Key Dependency

Travel booking depends on having the final location, dates, and attendee list.

## 4 to 6 Weeks Before

### Build the Experience

- Book attendee travel
- Confirm ground transportation
- Select team activity
- Reserve restaurants
- Draft the leadership agenda
- Confirm catering options
- Confirm AV needs
- Review budget against actual commitments

### Key Dependency

Catering, room setup, AV, and some transportation details depend on the final agenda and attendee information.

## 3 to 4 Weeks Before

### Close Information Gaps

- Confirm final attendee count
- Finalize dietary restrictions
- Finalize agenda
- Confirm room setup
- Confirm AV requirements
- Review all open vendor items
- Prepare attendee information packet
- Check remaining contracts and payment deadlines

### Escalate if Needed

Bring leadership any unresolved decisions involving:

- Budget changes
- Contract exceptions
- Agenda conflicts
- Major vendor issues
- Significant schedule changes

## 2 Weeks Before

### Final Confirmation Phase

- Send attendee information packet
- Reconfirm hotel rooming list
- Reconfirm transportation
- Reconfirm catering
- Reconfirm restaurants
- Reconfirm activity vendor
- Verify payments and remaining balances
- Prepare executive briefing
- Confirm emergency and after-hours vendor contacts

## 1 Week Before

### Stress Test the Plan

Review the full offsite from start to finish and ask:

- Does every attendee know where to go?
- Are arrival and departure details complete?
- Are all vendors confirmed?
- Are payment deadlines handled?
- Are dietary and accessibility needs covered?
- Are there any agenda gaps?
- Is there a backup plan for weather, transportation, or vendor issues?
- Is leadership waiting on any unresolved decisions?

Resolve anything that could become a day-of problem.

## 24 to 48 Hours Before

### Final Readiness Check

- Confirm attendee arrivals
- Confirm venue setup
- Confirm catering headcount
- Confirm transportation timing
- Confirm printed or digital materials
- Confirm executive schedule
- Confirm vendor contact list
- Review contingency plans

## During the Offsite

### Execution

- Monitor vendor arrivals and setup
- Keep the schedule moving
- Handle attendee questions
- Adjust logistics as needed
- Track changes and decisions
- Protect leadership from unnecessary operational interruptions
- Resolve issues directly when executive input is not required

## After the Offsite

### Closeout

- Reconcile invoices and remaining payments
- Confirm all vendor obligations are complete
- Collect feedback
- Document lessons learned
- Capture follow-up actions from leadership sessions
- Update the process for the next event

## Why This Structure Works

The goal is to move decisions forward in the right order.

I focus first on the items that unlock other work, then move into logistics, confirmation, and contingency planning.

That keeps the project organized and reduces the chance that a small delay becomes a larger last-minute issue.
